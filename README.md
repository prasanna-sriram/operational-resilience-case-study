# The Day the Workaround Failed: Operational Resilience

**A case-study project by Prasanna Sriram · Operational risk and resilience**

Can urgent claims intake and triage continue when the documented fallback has untested dependencies and capacity?

Cedarline’s recovery documents describe systems and workarounds. The case asks whether customers can actually report urgent claims, have their urgency recognized and reach an accountable next owner during disruption. A backup channel and manual intake process are examined against the failures they are supposed to address.

## Purpose

Operation boundaries, customer impact, recovery objectives, disruption tolerances, dependencies, controlled fallbacks, exercise evidence and limitations. The project uses fictional situations to connect framework concepts with evidence, trade-offs and accountable decisions.

## Fictional setting

**Cedarline Assurance** is a fictional federally regulated canadian property-and-casualty insurer. [Read the company profile](/company_profile.md) for the operation, stakeholders, assumptions and boundaries. People, events, suppliers and quantities are illustrative and do not refer to real businesses.

## Interpretation and evidence

Urgent claims intake starts as a candidate critical operation, not a classification automatically approved by the case author. Selected offline tests do not prove full-volume production recovery. Numerical examples are synthetic and are not regulatory default tolerances. The references support the framework context; proposed test methods and templates are illustrative analysis, not regulator-prescribed forms.

A source or worked example does not, by itself, demonstrate compliance. Distinguish a control’s design, observed operation, tested coverage and the authority to accept remaining risk. Source-review dates and licensed-text access limitations are recorded in the [source register](references/source_register.md).
