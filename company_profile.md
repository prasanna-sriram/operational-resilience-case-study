# Cedarline Assurance — Fictional Company Profile

[Project overview](/README.md)

## Scenario premise

A fictional federally regulated Canadian property-and-casualty insurer. This profile defines the learning scenario. No incorporation, registration, size, revenue, customer count or regulatory finding is asserted about a real organisation.

## Business situation

Cedarline’s recovery documents describe systems and workarounds. The case asks whether customers can actually report urgent claims, have their urgency recognized and reach an accountable next owner during disruption. A backup channel and manual intake process are examined against the failures they are supposed to address.

## Question under review

Can urgent claims intake and triage continue when the documented fallback has untested dependencies and capacity?

## Systems and information

The chapters introduce only the systems, flows and permissions needed for the decision. A diagram, record or numerical example is a fictional representation; missing facts remain unknown until explicitly supplied. Avoid treating illustrative details as a complete architecture or risk inventory.

## Scope and boundaries

Urgent claims intake starts as a candidate critical operation, not a classification automatically approved by the case author. Selected offline tests do not prove full-volume production recovery. Numerical examples are synthetic and are not regulatory default tolerances.

## Assessment approach

Start with the operation or intended use. Identify the failure path and potential impact, inspect proportionate evidence, evaluate response options, and state who can decide. Keep open issues and the limits of a test visible.

## Scenario maintenance

If a later case changes a purpose, dependency, permission or regulatory premise, state that change in the case and explain whether earlier conclusions still apply. Additions to the fictional setting are not changes to external law.
